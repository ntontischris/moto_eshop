# Payments build on Stripe now and launch card-acquiring on Viva, both behind one PaymentProvider

The contract (Article 6.7) names card, Apple Pay, Google Pay, Revolut, Klarna 0% and SEPA. Greek law additionally mandates **IRIS** instant payments for every e-shop since 1 Nov 2025 — not in the contract, but a go-live compliance obligation. No single processor cleanly covers all of this at the best price, and the originally-assumed provider (Viva) gates the whole payment milestone behind a KYC wait of ≥1 week.

We **introduce one `PaymentProvider` interface — mirroring [IErpAdapter] — and build two adapters behind it**. **Stripe is the build-and-unblock provider**: it self-serves a test key today (no KYC), and natively covers every contractually-named rail (cards, Apple/Google Pay, Klarna, Revolut, SEPA). This converts the project's #1 blocker into non-blocked work — the Phase 2 payment milestone can be built and demoed now. **Viva is the launch card-acquirer**: its all-in fee on Greek/EU consumer cards (~0.8–1.4% Interchange++) is materially below Stripe's 1.5% + €0.25 — at volume a ~0.75%-of-revenue difference — and it is the source of **IRIS**, which is just a payment-method toggle inside the same Viva Smart Checkout `create order → webhook verify` pipe (no separate DIAS integration). Viva also has a **demo account** (no KYC), so its adapter is built and tested now too; only the live-money flip waits on KYC.

The provider you **build** on is therefore deliberately not the provider you **launch** on — the fee difference only bites on live volume, and by go-live the abstraction re-points card-acquiring to Viva with zero rework. Stripe stays for the rails Viva is weak on (Klarna/Revolut) and as fallback.

Considered (a) **Viva only** — rejected: blocks the entire milestone behind KYC and is weaker on Klarna/Revolut/SEPA. (b) **Stripe only** — rejected: no IRIS (legally mandatory) and higher card fees at volume. (c) **charging the higher Stripe fee permanently for simplicity** — rejected: ~€7.5k/€1M of revenue is real margin. This is hard to reverse because the `PaymentProvider` contract threads through checkout, the [Order total], webhook reconciliation and order state; pricing stays server-authoritative regardless of provider — see [ADR 0001](./0001-server-authoritative-pricing.md).
