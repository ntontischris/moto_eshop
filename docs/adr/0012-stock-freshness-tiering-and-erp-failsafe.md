# Catalog is cached, stock is verified live only at commit, and an ERP outage keeps the shop selling

The [read-model](./0011-storefront-serves-from-supabase-read-model.md) raises one sharp question: if everything is served from Supabase, how do we avoid overselling stock we no longer have? Different data has different freshness needs, and conflating them under one "sync" is wrong — catalog text can be hours stale harmlessly, but selling a last unit that a physical store just sold is not acceptable.

We **tier freshness by data type**. **Catalog** (descriptions, categories, images, base price) is served from Supabase always, synced on a slow cadence — it never touches the ERP on browse. **Stock for display** (PLP/PDP badges) is served from Supabase too, synced on a frequent cadence (minutes), shown as indicative. **Stock at commit** (add-to-cart confirm and checkout) is the only place we call the ERP **live** (`AppStockStatus`): this is the moment of decision, it is low-volume relative to page views, and it is where overselling is actually prevented. The guarantee lives at the commit point, not on every render — so the ERP stays light and stock is still trustworthy when it matters.

When the ERP is unreachable **at commit** — a rare, short outage — two values conflict: "don't sell what you don't have" and the contract's "continuous operation even if ERP down" (Article 3.1). We choose to **keep selling**: fall back to last-known Supabase stock and accept the order, rather than block every customer for the duration. The oversell window is tiny (only during downtime, only units sold in the gap) and is resolved by apology/refund, which is cheaper than turning away all traffic during a rare outage. Orders are never pushed to the ERP synchronously: checkout writes the order to Supabase and an **order queue** worker pushes it to Entersoft (`AppCreateOrder`) with retry/backoff, so checkout never blocks on ERP availability and the [Order total] never depends on a live ERP call.

Considered (a) **live ERP stock on every product view** — rejected: it would crush the already-strained ERP. (b) **blocking checkout when the ERP can't confirm** — rejected: punishes every customer for a rare outage and contradicts Article 3.1. (c) **synchronous order push** — rejected: makes checkout fail whenever the ERP hiccups. This is hard to reverse because the commit-time check and the queue thread through cart confirmation, checkout and order state; pricing stays server-authoritative — see [ADR 0001](./0001-server-authoritative-pricing.md).
